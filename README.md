# The research behind Miles Cranmer's *How to avoid AI brain rot*

**Source index assembled October 17, 2026**  
**Talk:** https://x.com/MilesCranmer/status/2111105654211608847

I went through a higher-resolution recording and identified **13 research references** from the slides. They are collected below in the order in which they appear, with approximate timestamps and a sentence on what each source actually addresses. Some slide changes may have fallen between the 15-second screenshots, so this is **a recovered reading list, not a claim to be the lecturer's complete official bibliography**.

1. **02:30 — Pew Research Center (2026), AI chatbot use and attitudes by age.** [Original Pew report](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-use-of-ai-differ-by-age/). Survey of U.S. adults; an *ever used* figure is not a measure of AI proficiency or worldwide adoption.

2. **04:30 — Jiang et al. (NeurIPS 2025), *Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)*.** [Conference paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/754d5a526a5ee5a47220664a0eb92751-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html). Examines similarity in model outputs, not lasting changes in people's cognitive capacities.

3. **05:45 — Doshi & Hauser (2024), *Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content*.** [*Science Advances*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). AI ideas improved rated short-story outputs but increased similarity between stories; it does not test durable effects on writers.

4. **07:15 — Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2025), *Generative AI at Work*.** [*Quarterly Journal of Economics*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044). Studies productivity in customer support, with particularly strong gains for less-experienced workers; not a general measurement of intellectual formation.

5. **08:00 — Dell'Acqua et al. (2026), *Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier*.** [*Organization Science*](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838). Finds that AI-assistance outcomes depend on whether tasks fall within a model's capabilities.

6. **09:45 — Hitzig et al. (2026), *Agentic coding and persistent returns to expertise*.** [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise). Observational agent-coding analysis; apparent expertise correlates with success, but expertise and success were inferred from conversation data, not randomized.

7. **16:15 — Bastani et al. (2025), *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning*.** [*PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122). Unrestricted AI assistance increased practice scores but reduced scores on an immediate AI-free test; a safeguarded tutor avoided the detected loss **without exceeding the no-AI group's test performance**.

8. **17:00 — Shen & Tamkin (2026), *How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills*.** [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills). In a 52-developer randomized study, AI users scored lower on an immediate comprehension quiz; long-term skill decay was not tested.

9. **18:15 — Lehmann, Cornelius & Sting (revised 2025), *AI Meets the Classroom: When Do Large Language Models Harm Learning?*.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09047). The randomized comparisons showed **no overall learning effect**; associations of answer-seeking and explanation-seeking styles were exploratory, not separately randomized causal effects.

10. **19:15 — Deslauriers et al. (2019), *Measuring actual learning versus feeling of learning in response to being actively engaged in the classroom*.** [*PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116). Active instruction improved measured learning even while lowering perceived learning. Predates the current generation of LLMs.

11. **21:45 — Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Test-Enhanced Learning*.** [*Psychological Science*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x). Classic evidence for retrieval practice and delayed retention; does not directly test an AI tutor.

12. **23:30 — Kestin et al. (2025), *AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning*.** [*Scientific Reports*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6). A deliberately engineered physics tutor improved short-term post-lesson outcomes relative to the classroom condition; durable retention and which individual tutoring feature mattered remain unresolved.

13. **38:45 — Deming & Noray (2020), *Earnings Dynamics, Changing Job Skills, and STEM Careers*.** [*Quarterly Journal of Economics*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjaa021). Documents skill change in STEM labor markets before contemporary generative AI; not evidence that LLMs caused those changes.

**A caution about the bigger claim:** Better AI-assisted work, better learning while assisted, retained individual skill, and collective diversity of thinking are different outcomes. They cannot be treated as interchangeable. Several studies here examine short interventions; others describe workplace productivity, pre-LLM pedagogy, or model-generated content. Cranmer's talk is a useful synthesis, but the distinctions matter when citing the underlying evidence.

*This independent source index is not an official bibliography and is not endorsed by Miles Cranmer or the cited authors.*
